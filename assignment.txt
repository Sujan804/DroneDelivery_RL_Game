           Reinforcement Learning Game Assignments

Choose ONE of the 12 game assignments in this document. Develop a simple playable game, train one
reinforcement learning agent, and explain its performance. Each option carries 20 marks using the same
rubric.

What you need to do

    · Build or adapt a small 2D game with a visible objective, score, reset button or key, and basic human
         controls.

    · Define the agent's observations, actions, and reward. Use one suitable RL algorithm; suggested
         choices appear with each game.

    · Train the agent and provide a mode that loads and runs the trained policy. A saved Q-table is
         acceptable.

    · Test the trained agent for 10 episodes and a random-action agent for 10 episodes using the same
         starting scenarios. Report average reward and the game metric listed in your option.

    · Briefly explain one successful behavior, one limitation, and one possible improvement.

Keep the scope small

One level and simple shapes are sufficient. Use compact numerical observations rather than camera
images. Existing game environments, physics engines, and RL libraries are allowed if acknowledged. Explain
your own changes and the algorithm you used.
You do not need multiple RL algorithms, advanced graphics, online deployment, extensive parameter
searches, or multiple training seeds. Extra features do not earn additional marks beyond the rubric.

What to submit

    · Source code (python notebook), a saved policy or Q-table, and a short README with setup and run
         instructions.

    · A report of 2­3 pages containing your information (Assignment title, Roll, Name), the game
         objective, observations/actions/reward, algorithm and main settings, a training reward plot, the 10-
         episode comparison, and a short discussion.

    · A demonstration of about 3 minutes, live or recorded, showing human play and trained-agent play.
The following options describe the minimum game scope. All games are simulations; real hardware is not
required.

How to submit

Assignment 1 Lunar Lander

Land a spacecraft safely on a platform using limited fuel.
Real world connection: Autonomous landing and vehicle control.
Build: Use one flat landing pad, gravity, and a few discrete thruster controls. End an episode on landing,
crashing, or a time limit.
Observations: Position, velocity, tilt, and fuel.
Actions: Main thrust, left thrust, right thrust, or no thrust.
Suggested algorithm: DQN, or Q-learning with discretized observations.
Reward idea: Reward safe landing; penalize crashes and fuel use. Choose and explain simple numerical
values.
Report: Number of safe landings out of 10. Also report average episode reward for the trained and random
agents.

Assignment 2 Drone Delivery

Fly a drone around obstacles and reach a delivery zone.
Real world connection: Autonomous delivery.
Build: Use a small 2D map with two fixed obstacles and one target. End on reaching the target, collision, or
a time limit.
Observations: Drone position, velocity, and target direction.
Actions: Move left, right, up, down, or hover.
Suggested algorithm: DQN.
Reward idea: Reward reaching the target; penalize collisions and each time step. Choose and explain
simple numerical values.
Report: Successful deliveries out of 10. Also report average episode reward for the trained and random
agents.

Assignment 5 Traffic Commander

Control traffic lights to move cars through an intersection.
Real world connection: Traffic signal control.
Build: Use one intersection with two traffic phases. The game must enforce safe transitions. Each episode
lasts a fixed number of simulation steps.
Observations: Queue lengths and current signal phase.
Actions: Keep the current phase or request the other phase.
Suggested algorithm: Q-learning with grouped queue lengths, or DQN.
Reward idea: Reward cars crossing; penalize waiting queues. Choose and explain simple numerical values.
Report: Average vehicle waiting time across 10 episodes. Also report average episode reward for the
trained and random agents.

Observations: Distance from track center, heading error, and nearby track boundaries.
Actions: Steer left, steer right, or continue straight.
Suggested algorithm: DQN.
Reward idea: Reward forward progress; penalize leaving the track. Choose and explain simple numerical
values.
Report: Completed laps out of 10. Also report average episode reward for the trained and random agents.

Assignment 10 Power Grid Defender

Keep a small city supplied with electricity using a battery.
Real world connection: Energy storage management.
Build: Create a turn-based game with 24 turns, a finite battery, and a simple repeating supply and demand

Observations: Turn number, battery charge, supply, and demand.
Actions: Charge, discharge, or hold, within battery limits.
Suggested algorithm: Tabular Q-learning with discrete energy levels.

Assignment 11 Storekeeper Quest

Keep a game shop stocked and earn profit over a short season.
Real world connection: Inventory management.
Build: Create a turn-based shop with one product, limited storage, next-turn delivery, and random daily
demand. Each episode lasts 30 days.
Observations: Stock level and day number.
Actions: Order zero, a small quantity, or a large quantity.
Suggested algorithm: Tabular Q-learning.
Reward idea: Use daily sales revenue minus purchase, holding, and shortage costs. Choose and explain

Report: Average total profit across 10 episodes. Also report average episode reward for the trained and

Assignment 12 Recycling Rush

Move a sorting paddle to send falling items into the correct bins.
Real world connection: Automated waste sorting.
Build: Use two item categories and two bins. Present one item at a time with a limited number of steps
before it falls. Each episode contains 20 items.
Observations: Item category, paddle position, and time until the item falls.
Actions: Move left, move right, or stay.
Suggested algorithm: Tabular Q-learning.
Reward idea: Reward correct sorting; penalize incorrect or missed items. Choose and explain simple
numerical values.
Report: Percentage of items sorted correctly across 10 episodes. Also report average episode reward for
the trained and random agents.
